# Sources and checks: accessibility-for-small-business-2026

## Facts and the pages I actually opened

- WebAIM Million 2026 (data collected Feb 2026): 95.9% of home pages with detected WCAG 2 failures; 56.1 average errors per page; low contrast 83.9%, missing alt 53.1%, missing form labels 51%, empty links 46.3%, empty buttons 30.6%, missing document language 13.5%. https://webaim.org/projects/million/
- WCAG 2.2 became a W3C Recommendation on 5 October 2023; nine new criteria (2.4.11, 2.4.12, 2.4.13, 2.5.7, 2.5.8, 3.2.6, 3.3.7, 3.3.8, 3.3.9) with levels as listed; 4.1.1 Parsing removed as obsolete. https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/
- Contrast 4.5:1 and 3:1 for large text (18pt / 14pt bold); focus visible (2.4.7). https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/ (note: current document is dated 12 Dec 2024; the 5 Oct 2023 date comes from the "new in 2.2" page above). Only the first 100,000 characters were read. The 24 by 24 px figure for 2.5.8 comes from the "new in 2.2" page.
- Attorney-General's Department DDA review: open 1 Aug 2025, closed 14 Nov 2025, 15 Disability Royal Commission recommendations, "next stages" wording, no outcomes announced, no mention of digital accessibility or WCAG (page marked "Feedback updated 26 Oct 2026"). https://consultations.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/dda-issues-paper/
- Speedcubing Australia facts (filter chips keyboard-operable and announce state) from the site's own project file: src/content/projects/speedcubing-australia.md

- AHRC "Standards and guidelines for digital accessibility (2025)": latest version is WCAG 2.2 (October 2023); organisations should conform with WCAG 2.2 at a minimum Level AA and consider appropriate Level AAA criteria. https://humanrights.gov.au/?a=68429
- AHRC "Guidelines on equal access to digital goods and services" (April 2025): updates the 2014 World Wide Web Access advisory note; issued under s67(1)(k) DDA and s11(1)(n) AHRC Act 1986; not legally binding, to be read with the DDA; meeting the standards "will help organisations to meet their obligations" and they are "the minimum organisations should aim for"; says to meet the latest published WCAG version. https://humanrights.gov.au/our-work/disability-rights/publications/guidelines-equal-access-digital-goods-and-services
- DDA 1992 s24 (AustLII): unlawful to discriminate on the ground of disability in providing goods or services or making facilities available "whether for payment or not": refusal, terms or conditions, manner of provision. https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/dda1992264/s24.html
- DDA 1992 s11 (AustLII) unjustifiable hardship: all relevant circumstances, including benefit/detriment, effect of disability, financial circumstances and estimated expenditure, availability of assistance, action plans. https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/dda1992264/s11.html
- Directive (EU) 2019/882 (EUR-Lex): Art 2(2) listed services provided to consumers after 28 June 2025 (includes e-commerce); definitions of e-commerce services and service provider (includes making offers to consumers in the Union); Art 4(5) microenterprise services exemption; microenterprise = under 10 persons and turnover OR balance sheet total not over EUR 2 million. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj/eng
- EU business portal (Your Europe), read earlier: applies after 28 June 2025; disproportionate burden needs evidence reviewed every 5 years; its summary mentions only turnover for the small-business exemption, so the post follows the Directive text instead. https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/selling-in-eu/selling-goods-services/accessibility/index_en.htm
- European Commission news item: e-commerce platforms listed as covered. https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/eu-becomes-more-accessible-all-2025-07-31_en
- European Commission EAA page: e-commerce on the list; nothing on non-EU businesses. https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/policies/justice-and-fundamental-rights/disability/union-equality-strategy-rights-persons-disabilities-2021-2030/european-accessibility-act_en

All pages above were opened in a real browser (the AHRC, AustLII and EUR-Lex pages on 2026-11-09).

## Unsure / please check

- "Level AA is the level most business sites aim for" is general knowledge, not sourced.
- "I haven't found an Australian statute or regulation that itself requires WCAG 2.2" is a statement of absence. Search results mentioned vendor blogs claiming DDA amendments passed in Nov 2025 referencing WCAG 2.1 AA, and a Digital Inclusion Standard for government agencies (WCAG 2.1 vs 2.2 conflicting). I did not verify either, so left them out. Worth checking the Federal Register of Legislation yourself for any 2025-26 amendments.
- Whether and how EU national authorities enforce the Directive against non-EU sellers: NOT verified. The post says an Australian seller offering to EU consumers can fit the "service provider" definition and tells the reader to ask an adviser.
- Courts: nothing opened says how a court would treat the AHRC guidelines. The post says so and makes no claim either way.
- The 2.4.11 plain-English gloss ("can't cover it completely") paraphrases "at least partly visible".
