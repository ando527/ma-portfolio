A developer telling you that you need a developer is about as neutral as a baker telling you that you need bread.

So here is where I think AI website builders are good enough, and where they aren't.

Good enough: a one-page site for a side project, a club, a market stall, or an event page you'll switch off in three months. If a mistake costs you an afternoon, use the tool.

Where they fall over:

WebAIM's 2026 scan of one million home pages found 95.9% had detectable accessibility failures, and errors per page rose 10.1%. The report says AI-assisted coding likely plays a part.

Security gets serious once you take logins or customer details. A Lovable-built app reported in February 2026 exposed 18,697 user records.

And if you're replacing an old site that ranks on Google, the AI doesn't know which old addresses need redirecting. You do, or someone you hire does.

My rough test: what happens if the site is wrong for a month? If the answer is "not much", build it yourself. If it takes payments, personal details or carries your brand, get a person to at least review it.
