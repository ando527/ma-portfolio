95.9% of the top million home pages have detectable accessibility failures, and the average page has 56 of them.

That's from WebAIM's 2026 report, and most of those errors are the same few things:

Text that's too faint to read (83.9% of pages)
Images with no alt text (53.1%)
Form fields with no label (51%)

None of these need a rebuild. Most are an afternoon's work: darken a grey, name a button, add a proper label to the contact form.

I've written a plain-English guide for Australian small businesses covering:

- what WCAG 2.2 actually asks for
- where the Disability Discrimination Act stands (the Human Rights Commission's guidelines call WCAG 2.2 AA the minimum to aim for but aren't legally binding, and I couldn't find any announced change to website rules)
- whether the European Accessibility Act applies if you sell into the EU (microenterprises have an exemption, and how it applies to an Australian seller is unclear)
- six cheap fixes, in the order I'd do them

I'm a web developer, not a lawyer, so the legal parts are background and not advice.

A quick test you can do right now: put your mouse down and press Tab through your own homepage. If you lose track of where you are, your visitors do too.
